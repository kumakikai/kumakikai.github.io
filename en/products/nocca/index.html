Nocca

iPhone

In development

Stay in touch with family.At your own pace.

For people who find conversation or contact with family overwhelming. Share just what you need to say, while family members receive your message and reply when needed.

Screen from the app in development

Screenshots show the Japanese version.

Let the person communicating set the pace.

There can be things you want to tell your family even when meeting face to face or writing a long message feels difficult. Nocca is being developed for communicating those needs through short signals.

The person expressing their wishes at their own pace uses the main role; the family role receives those signals and replies when needed. The person communicating chooses who to connect with and when to talk.

Key features

  • Short words, selected with an icon

    Choose signals such as “I’d like food” or “I can’t talk right now.” Words and icons can be added, edited and reordered to suit the person.

  • Brief replies from family

    Family members choose from short replies set by the person communicating. A signal and its reply are designed to appear on one card.

  • Open a conversation when ready

    The person communicating opens a short conversation with “Talk for now” and decides when to close it. They can configure requests to talk from family.

  • Control how to stay connected

    The person communicating approves family connections and controls muting individual family members, pausing communication and disconnecting.

Choose one thing to communicate

This development screen offers short signals about meals, daily needs and how someone feels, providing a starting point without composing a message from scratch.

Understand each role at the start

Onboarding explains the two roles: the person communicating their wishes and the family member receiving them. Each chooses the corresponding entry point.

For people and their families

  • When face-to-face conversation feels difficult

    For moments when someone needs to tell family something but finds speaking in person burdensome.

  • When a long message is hard to write

    For choosing a prepared, short phrase to communicate a need or how the day feels.

  • For family who want to respect that pace

    For family members who want to receive the person’s signals and respond briefly.
